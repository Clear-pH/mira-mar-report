Executive Summary

More Volume · Same Price · Quality Lags

The first full month at the higher budget bought more people, not cheaper ones. Google and Meta spend rose 33% to $32.4K and the CRM recorded 125 genuinely new contacts against 80 the month before (+56%), with paid Google up 38% (16 to 22) and Meta up 32% (41 to 54). LinkedIn, live only since October 28, added 24 more in its first week. Cost per CRM-verified lead from Google and Meta held flat at about $427, so the extra money scaled volume at the same price rather than buying efficiency.

Quality is where the month falls short: 12 contacts are rated good today versus 10, and Meta produced just 1 of them from 62. Paid Google produced 3 and the sales center 3. 39 contacts from this window have not been worked yet, so those quality numbers will move.

On creative, the new 'Day at Mira Mar' video earned 7 leads from 5,192 views, the best rate of any video in the account, while the Yoga cuts never got enough delivery to judge. Two lines spent with nothing to show: YouTube ($1,609, zero clicks) and Display ($2,101, zero good leads). Phone calls fell from 49 to 16, almost all on Google, and the answer rate stayed at 56%.

Two ways to count a lead

Cost per lead is spend divided by leads. This report shows it two ways. The platform view divides spend by the leads Google and Meta report themselves: 129 this month, $251 each. Platform-reported: 39 Google Ads conversions + 90 Meta leads. Spark (the CRM, the source of truth for real people) recorded 125 genuinely new non-agent contacts in the same 30 days (138 before removing the Oct 15 back-fill), 100 of them from paid Google, Meta or LinkedIn.

On Oct 15 the sales team pushed 13 older incomplete-form leads (Jul–Oct) into Spark in one batch; they carry Oct 15 creation dates but are not new demand. They are removed from the 'new demand' figures below (identified by clustered 10:39/10:47 timestamps; inference, not matched against the push log). All 13 are Non Responsive or Not A Buyer.

Google Ads

Search Healthy · Display & YouTube Not

Google spent $18,000.57, up 25.6%, and returned 39 conversions against 40, so its platform cost per lead rose 28.8% to $461.55. Clicks rose to 4,342 from 4,036, but cost per click climbed 16.9% to $4.15 and the blended conversion rate slipped to 0.90% from 0.99%. Behind the 39 are 28 website form submissions (36 last month) and 11 phone-call leads (29). The CRM tells a better story than the platform: Spark counts 22 genuinely new paid-Google contacts against 16, up 38%, and paid Google produced 3 of the month's 12 good leads. Search itself is working: search-only click-through is 7.68%, and Branded search is the cheapest line in the account at $60.18 on 10 conversions. The weak spots are outside search. Display took 1,112,230 impressions and $2,100.67 for 5 conversions and no good leads, and 7 of the 11 call leads came from Display, 5 of them with no call duration. YouTube spent $1,608.98 across two campaigns with zero clicks. Within search, Non-Branded US fell from 8.5 conversions at $233.81 to 4 at $778.79, and Non-Branded Sarasota from 12.5 at $446.84 to 7 at $523.16; the new US-FL Keywords campaign added 6 at $402.18. Conversions now come mostly from computers (58.6%), and clicks skew 65+.

New creative · the question this month
Is the new creative outperforming the old? Day at Mira Mar is. Yoga hasn't been tested.

Four ads entered rotation this window: Video_Day at Mira Mar, its v2, Video_Yoga and Yoga v2. Day at Mira Mar produced 7 leads from 5,192 views — 1.35 leads per 1,000 views, the best rate of any video (Sarasota Lifestyle 0.75, Amenity 0.79). The Yoga and v2 cuts barely delivered (224 views combined), so they have not been tested yet. Of the established ads, Amenity jumped from 4 to 16 leads and the Building brochure image from 15 to 23.

Meta's creative table reports leads, video views and engagements but no spend per ad, so the fair comparison is leads per 1,000 video views. On that basis the new video converts viewers at nearly twice the rate of the two established workhorses. The caveat is scale: 7 leads is an early read, and Last Great Address and Patrick reach 0.97 on just 2 leads each.
